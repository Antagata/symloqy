# The method: Star Map Mode, not mastery

## The idea

Most vocabulary tools treat a word like a level to be beaten: learn the definition, pass the quiz, mark it "mastered". That trains **recognition**. You understand the word when you meet it, but it rarely turns up when you are speaking.

Symloqy assumes the opposite. **A word is never finished, only more accessible.** Each word is a star in a constellation of meanings, sounds, origins, images and memories. You don't conquer it. You **orbit** it, and every encounter shows it from a new angle until it becomes part of how you think and speak.

## The aim behind it

The aim is to own your language. Precise words are tools for thinking clearly. There is a vocabulary in everyone that is already half-known, and practice brings it to the surface. The method is built for many short sessions a day, spoken aloud. That makes it the opposite of a weekly study block.

## Three levels of familiarity

| Level | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Recognition** | You understand the word when you hear or read it. |
| **Recall** | You can explain it without help. |
| **Production** | You reach for it naturally, in spontaneous speech. |

The long-term target is production, for the words that matter most. Scheduled recall keeps the rest within reach. Nobody can produce hundreds of words on a schedule, and the method doesn't pretend otherwise.

## The angles of an orbit

Each encounter with a word picks one or two of these:

- **Retrieval before explanation.** Try first. Then a clue, then the first letter, then the etymology, and only then the answer. The effort of retrieving is part of learning.
- **Your own sentence, about your real life.** It gets corrected honestly as *correct*, *understandable but awkward*, or *incorrect*, and never approved just to keep momentum.
- **Semantic contrast.** Pull apart near-synonyms such as *sardonic / sarcastic / cynical* or *gourmet / gourmand*, and choose the right one for a situation.
- **Etymology as a memory anchor.** Only established origins are used, and uncertain ones are flagged.
- **Stories.** Several studied words go into one short story with characters and tension. Then each word is recalled from the story, and the learner retells part of it.
- **Spoken rehearsal.** A long sentence is split into clauses, rehearsed, rebuilt, then varied.
- **Rhetoric.** Short exercises with devices (anadiplosis, paralipsis, periphrasis), analogies, fallacies and 30-second explanation challenges. Eloquence is precision, not ornament.
- **Connection.** A word is linked to a word from the same week, or the learner recalls a sentence they saved earlier from the word alone.

## Matching effort to the word

| Review type | For | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Fast recall (word → meaning, meaning → word, alternating) | most due words | about 30 s each |
| Contrast or story | words that belong together | one exercise covers several words |
| Production challenge (the word isn't named; the conversation is steered to a situation where it fits) | 2–3 words per session | a few minutes |

## Grading

Every review gets one private grade, based on the first attempt of the day only.

- **PRODUCED**: used unprompted, or used with a real person since the last session
- **RECALLED**: retrieved without help
- **HESITANT**: needed any clue
- **FORGOTTEN**: had to be told

The scheduler turns these grades into due dates (1 → 3 → 7 → 14 → 30 days). After that, a word joins the **orbit pool**, from which surprise words come back now and then. See [../voice-srs/scheduler_routine_spec.md](../voice-srs/scheduler_routine_spec.md).

## Best sentences

Good sentences are saved, but sparingly: at most three per session, and only once they have been refined. They come back later as recall prompts ("here is the word; what was your sentence?"). That ties vocabulary to the learner's own voice.

## What the coach is not

The coach is not a schoolteacher, and it is not a repetitive app. Turns are short, the learner talks most, and there are no grammar lectures, no "clean!" after every sentence and no forced repetition.

> Vocabulary is the raw material. Reasoning provides the structure. Rhetoric gives it force. Storytelling gives it life. Repetition makes it accessible.
